# Financial Risk Assessment Knowledge Base

## Concept: Profit Margin and Profitability
Profit margin measures the percentage of revenue that turns into net profit.
Formula: (Net Profit / Revenue) * 100
- A Profit Margin > 15% is considered strong (LOW risk under project criteria).
- A Profit Margin between 5% and 15% is moderate (MEDIUM risk under project criteria).
- A Profit Margin < 5% indicates weak earning power or cost pressures (HIGH risk under project criteria).
Significance: Profitability provides a financial buffer against revenue fluctuations and enables reinvestment, debt servicing, and sustainable operations.

## Concept: Debt-to-Asset Ratio and Financial Solvency
The Debt-to-Asset ratio evaluates the proportion of a company's total assets financed through debt obligations.
Formula: (Total Debt / Total Assets) * 100
- A Debt-to-Asset ratio < 40% indicates conservative leverage (LOW risk under project criteria).
- A Debt-to-Asset ratio between 40% and 60% indicates moderate leverage (MEDIUM risk under project criteria).
- A Debt-to-Asset ratio > 60% signals high leverage and potential solvency stress (HIGH risk under project criteria).
Significance: Solvency measures long-term viability. High leverage increases financial risk during economic downturns due to fixed debt payment obligations.

## Concept: Current Ratio and Short-Term Liquidity
The Current Ratio measures a company's ability to cover short-term liabilities with short-term assets.
Formula: Current Assets / Current Liabilities
- A Current Ratio >= 1.5 indicates healthy short-term liquidity (LOW risk under project criteria).
- A Current Ratio between 1.0 and 1.49 indicates moderate liquidity (MEDIUM risk under project criteria).
- A Current Ratio < 1.0 implies that short-term liabilities exceed short-term assets, causing liquidity distress (HIGH risk under project criteria).
Significance: A current ratio below 1.0 means the firm may struggle to pay immediate debts without selling long-term assets or securing external funding.

## Concept: Quick Ratio and Immediate Liquidity
The Quick Ratio (Acid-Test Ratio) measures immediate liquidity by excluding illiquid assets like inventory and prepaid expenses.
Formula: (Current Assets - Inventory - Prepaid Expenses) / Current Liabilities
- A Quick Ratio >= 1.0 means immediate liquid assets fully cover short-term liabilities (LOW risk under project criteria).
- A Quick Ratio between 0.5 and 0.99 indicates moderate quick liquidity (MEDIUM risk under project criteria).
- A Quick Ratio < 0.5 highlights reliance on inventory sales to meet immediate cash needs (HIGH risk under project criteria).
Significance: Quick ratio is a stricter measure of liquidity than the current ratio because inventory cannot always be liquidated quickly without price discounts.

## Concept: Debt-to-Equity Ratio and Leverage Structure
The Debt-to-Equity ratio compares total financial obligations against shareholders' equity.
Formula: Total Debt / Shareholders' Equity (where Equity = Total Assets - Total Debt)
Significance: Measures capital structure leverage. High debt-to-equity means creditors provide more financing than owners.
Note: In this assessment project, Debt-to-Equity is displayed as an informational metric but excluded from the composite risk score to avoid double-counting leverage already captured in the Debt-to-Asset ratio.

## Concept: Operating Cash Flow Ratio and Cash Generation
The Operating Cash Flow (OCF) Ratio compares cash generated from core business operations against short-term liabilities.
Formula: Operating Cash Flow / Current Liabilities
- An OCF Ratio >= 0.5 indicates strong operational cash generation relative to obligations (LOW risk under project criteria).
- An OCF Ratio between 0.2 and 0.49 indicates moderate cash coverage (MEDIUM risk under project criteria).
- An OCF Ratio < 0.2 indicates weak operational cash flow, making the firm reliant on external financing (HIGH risk under project criteria).
Significance: Accounting net profit can be affected by non-cash items; operating cash flow measures actual cash generated by business activities.

## Concept: Interest Coverage Ratio and Debt Service Ability
The Interest Coverage Ratio measures how easily a firm can pay interest expenses on outstanding debt using earnings before interest and taxes (EBIT).
Formula: EBIT / Interest Expense
- An Interest Coverage Ratio >= 5.0 indicates comfortable interest coverage (LOW risk under project criteria).
- An Interest Coverage Ratio between 2.0 and 4.99 indicates moderate coverage (MEDIUM risk under project criteria).
- An Interest Coverage Ratio < 2.0 signals severe debt service pressure (HIGH risk under project criteria).
- If interest expense is zero, risk is classified as LOW since there are no interest obligations.
Significance: Low interest coverage indicates that minor declines in operating earnings could lead to default on interest obligations.

## Concept: Multi-Factor Risk Assessment Methodology
Risk Scoring Framework:
- Each of the 6 evaluated risk categories (Profitability, Debt, Liquidity, Quick Liquidity, Cash Flow, Interest Coverage) receives 1 point for LOW risk, 2 points for MEDIUM risk, and 3 points for HIGH risk.
- Maximum possible risk score = 18 points.
- Overall Risk Classification:
  - Total Score <= 9 (<= 50% of max): LOW Overall Risk.
  - Total Score <= 13 (<= 75% of max): MEDIUM Overall Risk.
  - Total Score >= 14 (> 75% of max): HIGH Overall Risk.
Important Disclaimer: These criteria are project-specific assessment thresholds designed for structured risk evaluation, not universal accounting standards or professional investment advice.
